“I love to paint children,” Mary Cassatt once said. “They are so natural and truthful. They have no arrière-pensée (ulterior motive).” The American-born Impressionist became one of the most influential artists of 19th-century France with her refreshingly tender portraits of mothers and children painted in loose, tactile brushstrokes.
